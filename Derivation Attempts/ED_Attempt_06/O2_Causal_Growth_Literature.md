## The question

**Does anyone have a growth rule for a finite-neighbour causal order** that gives a smooth, space-like pattern **without building the dimension into its pieces?**

## What's known (C87)

| | what it says | source |
|---|---|---|
| **A framework for finite-neighbour causal rules** | "Causal graph dynamics": graphs of bounded degree that change over time, with information moving at a bounded speed and the same rule applied everywhere. Reversible versions keep their size (all but finitely many graphs), so **growth needs an irreversible rule** | Arrighi and Dowek, *Inf. Comput.* (2013); reversible CGD, arXiv:1502.04368 |
| **Causal invariance gives relativity-like behaviour** | In hypergraph rewriting, requiring that update order doesn't matter (causal invariance) gives a discrete analogue of Lorentz and general covariance. Dimension is estimated with causal-set methods (Myrheim–Meyer), but **it depends on the rule chosen** | Gorard, arXiv:2004.14810; arXiv:2011.12174 |
| **Finite neighbours mean a rest frame** | No finite-valency graph can be attached to a frame-free random spacetime pattern | Bombelli, Henson, Sorkin (2009), gr-qc/0605006 |
| **Causal 2D growth is a random tree** | Every two-dimensional causal triangulation corresponds one-to-one to a tree. The uniform infinite causal triangulation equals a critical random tree conditioned to survive. **Hausdorff dimension 2; spectral dimension at most 2** (exactly 2 in reduced versions) | Durhuus, Jonsson, Wheater, arXiv:0908.3643; arXiv:1203.2869 |
| **Trees and topology change** | Allowing space to change topology in 2D CDT maps to different tree ensembles | "Trees and spatial topology change in CDT" (listing) |
| **Comparisons** | CDT and causal sets have both been studied for how dimension emerges, including dimensional reduction at small scales in manifold-like causal sets | Glaser and Surya; arXiv:1710.00938 |

## On paper (C88)

### 1. There's a framework but no rule

- **Arrighi and Dowek's causal graph dynamics is the right formal home for what ED needs:** finite neighbours, bounded speed, one rule everywhere.
- **It's a language, not a result.** It says nothing about which rules give space-like patterns.
- **One consequence matters:** a reversible rule can't grow a pattern. ED's growth needs its arrow of time, and ED has one.

### 2. What 2D CDT shows exactly

- **In two dimensions, "space is a line that never splits" turns growth into a critical random tree:** each point of one slice has some number of children in the next.
- **The result is honestly two-dimensional:** one space direction plus time, with a walk that behaves (spectral dimension at most 2).
- **So causality with no branching does give smoothness.** But the slices were **given** as lines. The dimension of space was put in through the slices, not chosen by the rule.
- **That confirms note 15's split** (C84):
  - time order and no branching handle **roughness**;
  - the **dimension** of the slices has to come from somewhere else. In ED that would be the sync floor and commitment's push (C33).

### 3. Nobody has the combination ED needs

- **CDT gets four dimensions** by building with four-dimensional pieces.
- **The Wolfram model's dimension** depends on the rule chosen.
- **Causal sets** have no dynamics that picks space-like orders, and need infinite neighbours for relativity.
- **Nothing found does all of these at once:**
  - finite neighbours;
  - causal, with no branching of space;
  - no dimension built in;
  - a smooth pattern that stays.

**That's the open problem ED's growth question lands in.**

## What it means (C88)

- **The time-order reframing holds up.** Causality is the known cure for rough, branchy geometry, and 2D CDT shows it exactly.
- **It doesn't come with a ready-made rule.** The literature has no finite-neighbour causal growth rule that picks a space dimension.
- **ED's picture would need two parts that no one has joined:**
  - no-branching causal growth for smoothness;
  - sync plus commitment for three.
- **A third model (M3) would be original modelling, not borrowing.** There's no success in the literature to anchor it, and it would follow two failed models. The forking-paths risk is high, so I'm not proposing it now.
- **Verdict shape:** consistent, not derived. The growth wall is now stated as a precise open problem. Inputs are unchanged (still 3).
