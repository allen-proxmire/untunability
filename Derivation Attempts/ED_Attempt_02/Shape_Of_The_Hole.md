# The shape of the hole

*ED\_Attempt\_02, note 1. 2026-09-14. Not a result. A map of where a missing piece could live, drawn from what attempt 1 had to guess. Ledger: `01\_Ledger` (C1–C6, D1–D3). Attempt 1 references are marked A1-ledger (for example A1-ledger C303).*

## 

## 1\. Why so many attempts look plausible



**Physics at the scales we can measure is forgiving about what's underneath.** Very different microscopic rules end up looking the same once you zoom out. Physicists call this *universality*:



* Almost any rule for random hops gives ordinary spreading.
* Almost any quantum walk on a grid looks like the Dirac equation from far away.
* Almost any consistent theory of long-range attraction carried by a massless field ends up as general relativity.



**So known physics is like a valley that everything rolls into.** Attempt 1 rolled in step by step: its gravity became Einstein's, its dark energy a plain constant, its measurement ordinary decoherence. The decisions weren't wrong. Most were forced by measurements, and each one pushed ED further into the valley.



**Matching the valley barely tells you which rule is underneath.** That's why ED and its cousins (causal sets, cellular-automaton pictures, emergent-gravity programs) all reach "plausible". Plausible is cheap.

## 

## 2\. Where the missing piece would have to be



If everything rolls into the same valley, the missing piece can't be in the parts that roll. It has to be in the things that don't wash out:



* **Specific numbers:** particle masses, the strengths of the forces, the size of Λ.
* **Specific choices:** three dimensions, three generations of particles, left over right, matter over antimatter.



Look at what attempt 1 had to put in by hand:



|guessed or imposed|what kind of thing|
|-|-|
|3 dimensions|a specific choice|
|The birth chance (10⁻⁶¹)|a specific number|
|GR's feedback weights; the moving/ticking split|specific structure, fitted to data|
|The standing phase for handedness|a specific direction (an arrow in the phases)|
|Draw probabilities|a rule assumed, not explained|
|The coin, the lanes, the draw threshold|convenient defaults|



**The pattern is striking.** ED generated the generic stuff on its own: spreading, locality, one-way time, falling toward mass. Every specific thing, whether a number, a dimension or a direction, had to be inserted. That's true of the whole family, not just ED. None of these programs produces the electron's mass or explains why there are three generations.



**So the shape of the missing piece is not more dynamics, but a source of specificity.** Something that picks particular numbers and particular asymmetries. It could be a selection principle, a constraint, a boundary condition, or a special starting state.

## 

## 4\. What the free ones have in common



**The free inputs that matter** (leaving out the modelling conveniences):



1. **Three dimensions.**
2. **The birth chance's value.**
3. **The standing phase.**
4. **The meeting interaction.**
5. **The response strength.**
6. **The reasons behind** GR's weights and the Pythagorean split.



(The probabilities were on this list until note 2 showed they're forced given ED's structure.)



**None of them is dynamics.** Each is a particular *count*, *number* or *direction*, or the *reason* a particular structure holds.



**Most aren't local, either:**



* **Three dimensions** is a fact about the web's overall shape.
* **The birth chance** is a global rate.
* **The standing phase** is a property of a loop, not of a place.
* **The probabilities** are about how whole patterns relate.



**This matters because of the three kinds of "can't see it" (section 7).** These inputs are of the third kind: invisible at any one place, but shaping the whole.

## 

## 5\. Relation – gradient – boundary: sorting the free inputs



Allen's triad (D1) is useful here as a way of sorting the free inputs. It is a working frame, not a result.



|class|what it's about|free inputs that fall here|
|-|-|-|
|**Relation**|How things fit together around loops and between patterns: phases, how amplitudes combine|The standing phase; the draw probabilities (how amplitudes combine into chances)|
|**Gradient**|Differences between neighbours: how strongly one place responds to the next|The response strength; the reasons behind GR's weights and the moving/ticking split|
|**Boundary**|Edges, wholes and starts: the horizon, the starting state, the overall shape|Three dimensions; the birth chance (the rate at which the web's edge grows); the draw's final boundary|



**Why it's more than labels.** Attempt 1's walls were all relation- and boundary-type:



* the hand needed a *loop* phase;
* the draw needed a *horizon*;
* the hand was chosen by the *state*.



**What would count.** A principle would earn its place by tying several free inputs together, for example one boundary fact fixing both the dimension and the birth rate, or one relation fact fixing both the standing phase and the probabilities. A principle that fixes only one input is just a renamed input.

## 

## 6\. The shape under it all



**Allen's picture (D2).** Picture every free input as one direction in a space.



* **Measurements and theorems cut regions away.** What's left is a shape, and its dimension is the number of free inputs.
* **Scale:** attempt 1 has about five to seven physics-level free inputs. The Standard Model has 19, rising to about 26–28 with neutrino masses (C1).



**What a missing principle would look like in that picture.** The shape would be thinner than it looks: a surface of lower dimension running through it, where one or more directions aren't really free.



**Koide's relation is a real hint of exactly that kind** (C3):

* **The relation:** for the three charged leptons, (sum of masses) ÷ (sum of square roots of masses)² = 2/3, to current precision, and it has never been derived.
* **Brannen's form:** the square roots of the masses sit at three points 120° apart on a circle, √m\_k ∝ 1 + √2 cos(δ + 2πk/3). The 2/3 depends only on the depth √2; the angle δ ≈ 2/9 sets the ratios.
* **In the picture:** one direction in the three-mass space isn't free.

**What Allen's own Koide work found** (ED Generative notes, 2026-09-05 to 09-08):

* **Re-expressing Koide in ED's words transmits nothing.** Each reading needed a substitution chosen to hit the known number, and two such readings contradicted each other, so both were withdrawn.
* **The standing verdict** there states the hole's shape from the mass side: *ED's primitive structure produces classifications and dichotomies cleanly, but does not produce continuous numerical relationships.*
* **The √2 note asks the right kind of question.** Nature sits at an interior point of its allowed range: Koide's 2/3 inside \[1/3, 1), and the quantum correlation limit 2√2 inside \[2, 4]. *Why stop short, and why there?* That is a question about specificity.

**Tao's exponent database as a method** (C4).

* **What it does:** Tao, Trudgian and Yang collected the known relations between exponents in analytic number theory, as code, and systematically optimized across them. That improved several best-known bounds without new theory.
* **What it doesn't do:** the project's own description gives no explicit geometric shape. The "region carved by relations" is an analogy.
* **A physics version would be a small database of inputs and relations:** measurements, no-go theorems, and empirical relations like Koide. It would map the region. It wouldn't explain it; the explanation would be whatever makes the region thin.



**Can the shape be worked out from the rest?** Not directly: universality hides it. But its outline can be drawn from the constraints (section 8).

## 

## 7\. Three kinds of "can't see it"



|kind|example|could it be the missing piece?|
|-|-|-|
|**Too small to measure yet**|The clock jitter that collapse models imply (A1-ledger C204, C205)|**Yes.** It just hasn't been reached|
|**Never observable, even in principle**|The overall phase of a wave; pure bookkeeping|**No.** Something that never shows up can't fix anything we see|
|**Invisible locally, shaping globally**|Loop phases, topology, boundaries, the starting state|**Yes.** This is where attempt 1's walls were|

## 

## 8\. The outline of the hole, so far



**What the missing piece must be:**



1. **A source of specificity, not more dynamics:** it picks numbers, counts and directions.
2. **Invisible locally, shaping globally,** like loops, boundaries and the state.
3. **Non-local or context-dependent,** if it's any kind of hidden ingredient (Bell's theorem; Gleason and related results).
4. **Free of preferred directions in space.** Discrete webs are tightly constrained here, and attempt 1 never checked it (A1-ledger T6). It is the hardest known wall for the whole family.
5. **One principle, several inputs.** It has to fix several free inputs at once, or it's just one more input.
