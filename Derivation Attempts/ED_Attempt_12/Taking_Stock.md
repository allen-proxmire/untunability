# Taking stock of attempt 12

*ED_Attempt_12, note 10. 2026-09-23 (RD13). Ledger: C13. Reasoning only; nothing computed. Written plainly.*

## The one-paragraph version

Attempt 12 opened on **persistence** — the one statement attempts 10 and 11 had both landed on, that a shape is carried rather than chosen. It turned out to be wrong, and attempt 12 is the record of finding that out properly. Switching ED's sync requirement **on** erases every shape instead of carrying it. Then, one at a time, three assumptions of mine were replaced by things ED actually says — no shared now, relations free to dissolve, commitment persisting — and each time the answer improved in character and stayed the same in kind. **ED's rules converge. They do not select.** From a line, a lattice or a random web, the pattern ends in the same place: sparse, spread out, holding its rates patch by patch, with no dimension.

## The standing statement

**ED's rules converge; they do not select.**

That is stronger than the persistence claim attempt 11 thought it had, and it is ED's own doing: three very different starts reach one common end state, tightly, with nothing tuned. But the end state is not space. It is sparse, it spreads out, it holds its rates locally, and it has no dimension.

## What Allen's interventions were worth, since he asked

Stated plainly because it is part of the record, not as encouragement.

- **The audit challenge** (A11 D9, "ED doesn't need space") exposed that four attempts had been testing a borrowed picture of space with **none of ED's clocks in it**. Road N and everything since exists because of it.
- **D5** ("synced doesn't imply a shared now") caught Claude having built a global lock into the filter and then reporting that ED destroys space. **That was a negative result about Claude's assumption, published as a negative result about ED.** It was caught from the meaning, in one sentence, with no numbers.
- **D4 and D6** (relations can dissolve; commitment persists) each changed the model's behaviour measurably — the second by a factor of six in churn.

**Three of the four largest corrections in this attempt came from Allen, and none of them came from the numbers.** That is the division of labour that has worked: Claude builds and measures; Allen catches formalisations that don't match what ED means.

## What attempt 12 cost, and what Claude got wrong

Ten notes, C1–C13, D1–D6, RD1–RD13, five programs, and roughly four hours of computing.

Claude's errors, all recorded before their results and all caught before anything was written up as a claim: the inheritance rule that inflated relations (a ring finishing on 12.6 per event against its starting 2, C4); a pass rule that charged the reading's own size drift to the pattern (C4); a ball that counted already-related events as candidates, so "reach 2" didn't mean reach 2 (C10); a dissolution rule that let relations go the instant they were idle (C11, corrected by D6); and the global lock (C8, corrected by D5) — the serious one.

**One result was withdrawn on its own check:** relations per event settled at 6.2–6.9 against ED's link budget of 6.699, which looked striking until it was tested at three pulls, moved, and was dropped within the hour (C5).

## What is open

**Not taken up in this attempt:** whether a relation's lapse should depend on more than carrying (C12 has no fourth candidate); what the common end state actually *is*, as an object in its own right; the two 2+1 options from attempt 11 (32 slices, the slice-size profile in the pass rule); roads R, I and D; amplitudes; A9's H3-Q2; A10's flagged flip-pairing reading.

**The question attempt 12 raises and does not answer:** ED's rules take every pattern to one place. **Nobody has looked at that place directly.** Everything so far asked whether it was three-dimensional space and answered no. What it *is* — reached from everywhere, sparse, spread out, holding its rates locally, chosen by nothing — has never been described.
