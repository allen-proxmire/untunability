## Road R — rates matching across the whole pattern

### Where it came from

Allen, while H1 was running: *"relativity is what I keep coming back to. It works great without a shared now... All ED says is that, of course there is a spot where clocks will tick the same. It doesn't assign any special property to that spot or those objects."* Then (D5): *"obviously, we have to go with: can match across the whole pattern."*

### What it says

**Sync means clocks can tick at the same rate across the whole pattern** — not that a slice shares one "now". Relativity needs no shared "now"; a shared one would be a preferred frame, which is ED's own wall 1.

### Why it's a road

**Attempt 8's road F tested the wrong thing.** It measured whether clocks **show the same time** — readings agreeing. ED claims only that they can **tick at the same rate**. Two watches can run at exactly the same rate and still show different times.

The physics literature separates the two: **rates matching needs more than 2 dimensions; readings matching needs more than 4** (Hong, Park and Choi). So road F held ED to the harder standard.

### What checking it would take

- **A reading for rates, not readings.** In the model's settled state every clock on a connected slice ticks at the common rate, so the test has to be sharper: **does rate-locking survive as the pattern grows**, or does it break down beyond some size? That's the version whose threshold is two dimensions.
- **Calibrate it** on a ring, a square grid and a cube grid, as before — rates should fail to lock across a growing ring, hold marginally on a square grid, and hold on a cube.
- **Then measure grown slices** — the H1 conditions-only slices first.

**It also keeps ED's reason for three dimensions**, since rates able to match across the whole pattern is attempt 6's argument (A6 G3), which gives three as the fewest.

## Road I — interactions need clocks within range

### Where it came from

Allen: *"chemical reactions have to be close together; cool down atoms and they behave as one thing... if you ignore space, maybe reactions and interactions need to be in a range of synchronization? Like you can't get an atom in orbit to react with one on the ground — not necessarily because of space, but at those rates perhaps they couldn't react?"*

### What it says

**Two things can only interact if their clocks tick at close enough rates.** Distance may matter less than it seems; what matters is being in step.

### Why it's a road — there's physics behind it

- **The Pound–Rebka experiment (1960)** put iron-57 atoms at the top and bottom of a 22.5-metre tower. The bottom atoms absorb the top atoms' gamma rays only when their ticks match almost exactly. **Gravity made the two heights tick at slightly different rates, and the absorption dropped** — distance unchanged. Moving one atom slowly to shift its rate back into step restored it. That is Allen's idea, measured.
- **Cold atoms lock into step and act as one thing** — a Bose–Einstein condensate.
- **Two coupled clocks lock only if their rates differ by less than the coupling can pull.** This is a standard, well-known result for coupled oscillators.

### ED may already contain the mechanism

**ED decided in attempt 6 that a single link can only pull a clock so far** (A6 D7). Two clocks whose rates differ by more than a link can pull **won't lock**. So *"interactions need clocks within range"* may already follow from ED's own rules, with no new ingredient.

### What checking it would take

- **On paper first:** does ED's bounded pull give a locking range, and how wide is it in ED's terms?
- **Then:** does that range connect ED's clock rates to **gravitational time dilation**, as Pound–Rebka does? If so, it would tie ED's sync to ED's account of gravity — and that would be the first bridge from the growth work to matter and gravity.

**This is about how matter interacts, not the shape of space**, so it doesn't bear on H1 or the growth wall directly.
