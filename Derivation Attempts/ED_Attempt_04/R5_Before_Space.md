## What ED said before: the ED Generative repo, read back (C28)

**The interpretation, from the essay "Turn It Twice":**
- **A particle is "a knot in a web, tethered."** It isn't a free point; it's connected to everything around it by threads of participation.
- **Spin ½** (two full turns to come home) is "the signature of being embedded in the web rather than floating free." That's the belt or plate trick: a tethered object needs two turns to untwist.
- **Pauli exclusion is "the tether's twist under a swap."** Swapping two identical tethered things is a kind of rotation, which gives a minus sign, which forbids sharing a state.
- **An antiparticle is "matter read against the grain of time."**
- **The essay's own honesty:** tether-spin is "a structural account, not a closed proof", and exclusion "leans on the standard exchange bookkeeping of quantum field theory".

**The papers** (102 spin–statistics, 103 frame uniqueness, 104 anyon prohibition, 110 commutation relations):
- **The fermion/boson split** came from three dimensions **postulated as a primitive** (P06), a phase on each carrier, and standard topology and field theory.
- **Spin values** were taken from standard physics.

**The repo's own outside review (2026-09-12):**
- **Anyon prohibition and frame uniqueness are "textbook result, restated".**
- **ED supplied no input beyond the assumed dimension.**
- **"Bosons and fermions only" holds for simple phases, not parastatistics.**

**So the old program found the same gap as R2: the dimension was assumed, not derived** (C33).

## What the literature has

| idea | what it says | ledger |
|---|---|---|
| **Causal sets: "order plus number equals geometry"** | The causal order of events fixes spacetime's shape up to scale, and counting elements gives the scale. Close to "time is the order of relations" | C26 |
| **Relationalism** | Leibniz: space is the order of things that coexist, time the order of succession. Barbour's shape dynamics is the modern version: only relational shape is physical. Close to "space is the pattern of relations" | C27 |
| **Quantum graphity; Page–Wootters** | Space from an all-connected start; time from correlations in a timeless whole (note 6) | C20, C21 |
| **Tethered objects can be fermions** | Finkelstein and Rubinstein (1968): extended objects (kinks, solitons) can carry half-integer spin, and spin–statistics follows from topology alone. **The known home for "tethered, so spin ½"** | C29 |
| **Three dimensions for stability** | Ehrenfest (1917): with the usual forces, stable orbits and atoms can't exist in more than 3 dimensions. Extended to general relativity and quantum mechanics (Tangherlini 1963), and discussed by Tegmark (1997). Critics question whether such arguments explain anything | C30 |
| **Knots need exactly three dimensions** | A loop can be knotted in 3 dimensions. **In 4 or more, every knotted loop can be untied**; in 2, loops can't knot at all | C31 |
| **Emergent dimension is hard** | Causal dynamical triangulations gets four dimensions at large scales, but about two near the Planck scale, and only in some phases of its settings | C32 |

**The pokes:**
- **Every piece of D is known mathematics or old physics.** Knots need 3D, particles-as-knots is a very old idea (Kelvin's vortex atoms, not checked here), and Ehrenfest-type arguments have critics. **What would be ED's own is only the combination:** "a particle is a knot of commitments" plus "commitments can't be undone".
- **Finkelstein–Rubinstein doesn't need knots.** Kinks and solitons are fermions without being knotted, so "knot" is an extra assumption beyond what spin ½ requires.
- **"Can't be undone" has to mean "can't be cut" for this to work.** Attempt 1 recorded commitments as permanent, but not in exactly this sense. That needs pinning down.
- **This explains why particles would be stable only in 3D. It doesn't show that space *grows* three-dimensional** from the first difference. Causal dynamical triangulations shows how hard that second step is.
- **Look-elsewhere:** several different arguments "select 3". Collecting ones that agree isn't evidence unless each was fixed first.

## What it means (C35)

- **The old ED interpretation of Pauli** (tethers, twist under a swap) **has a real home in physics** (Finkelstein–Rubinstein). The old papers restated the textbook results with three dimensions assumed.
- **R5's sharpest candidate is ED's own combination:** a particle as a knot of commitments that can't be cut. That would give a reason from ED's meanings why particles are stable only in three dimensions. It's a reason, not a derivation of emergent space, and every ingredient is known.
- **The known programs** (causal sets, relationalism, quantum graphity, Page–Wootters) already cover time as order and space as pattern. ED's additions are the first draw, sameness first, tethers as commitments, and uncuttable knots.
