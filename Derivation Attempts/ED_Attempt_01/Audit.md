# Audit: which choices are solid, which are shaky, and where to look

*2026-09-14. After G42 (RD40, back to constant births). Ledger: C224–C235, D14. Companion to [Stock_Take.md](Stock_Take.md).*

## 0. Why "it all held" doesn't mean the choices were right

**Allen:** "surprisingly, we have went back and adjusted a few times and it all held."

**Most adjustments held because they moved ED toward standard physics.**
- Standard physics is known to be consistent, so the pieces fitted together.
- **That shows ED is consistent. It doesn't show ED's own ideas are right.**
- Wherever ED said something different, a measurement pushed it back:
  - light bending, Mercury and the Earth–Moon wobble (gravity);
  - DESI distances (dark energy).

**So the honest question isn't "which choices were wrong".** It's which choices carry ED's own content, and which of those were never really tested.

## 3. The ED-I interpretations folder (C232)

**What's there:** 29 papers (February–March 2026), 3–15 pages each, covering superconductivity, spin, entanglement, the Higgs, neutrinos, photonics, black-hole scattering, galaxies and more.

**Your guess is right: mostly relabelling.** Almost every paper follows the same pattern: "In the ED ontology, X is not [the standard account]. It is [an ED-structure word]."

- **Almost none contains an equation.**
- **"Testable" appears seriously in only one** (ED-I-04). Even there the "predictions" are qualitative ("coherence will be sensitive to…"), with no sizes.

**Several make claims that experiments contradict:**

| paper | claim | what's measured |
|---|---|---|
| **ED-I-01** superconductivity | "not from electron pairing" | Trapped magnetic flux comes in units of h/2e. The 2 is the electron pair (Deaver–Fairbank and Doll–Näbauer, 1961, C228) |
| **ED-I-03** spin | "spin is not intrinsic angular momentum" | Spin angular momentum is handed to the crystal lattice and measured (ultrafast Einstein–de Haas, 2019, C231) |
| **ED-I-29** tunnelling | the particle doesn't "spend time inside" the barrier | The time spent inside was measured with a spin clock (Ramos et al., 2020, C229) |
| **ED-I-04** quantum–classical | atomic clock offsets arise "not from time dilation" | Clocks shift exactly by gravitational time dilation (C35, C38) |
| **ED-I-27** weak lensing | flat rotation curves are "not gravitational in origin" | Conflicts with the restart's own conclusion that ED's gravity is GR (RD37) |
| **ED-I-07** double slit | collapse happens when event density crosses a threshold | The restart tested this idea and dropped it (RD26) |

**ED-I-22** (the Local Group) presents a standard gravity simulation as "the first cosmological-scale empirical confirmation of ED". The simulation's own explanation, quoted in the paper, is ordinary gravity. Its "predictions" were written after the result.

**What to do with them:** treat them as a notebook of analogies, not physics claims, and don't circulate them as results. The one idea worth keeping in mind is ED-I-04's quantum–classical boundary. The restart has turned it into RD27 and RD28, which are testable and consistent.

## 4. The most promising place to look: handedness (C233)

**The one real result ED has is the handedness theorem:** mirror-symmetric transport can't have a net handedness.

**Nature is handed.** The weak force treats left and right differently. It was discovered in 1957 when cobalt-60 nuclei were shown to emit electrons preferentially in one direction (Wu et al., C224).

**So one of these must hold:**
1. **ED has a handed ingredient** after all. That contradicts A1, and it's where A1 should be reconsidered.
2. **Handedness appears spontaneously.** The mirror-symmetric rule settles into a handed state. This is the "chance" branch of Way Forward 1 (maximum uniformity, first difference).
3. **Handedness was in the starting conditions.**

**Why this is the best lead:**
- **It builds on ED's only real result,** rather than on something ED borrowed.
- **The Standard Model doesn't explain this either:** it puts the handedness of the weak force in by hand.
- **There is a large existing literature to check against first:**
  - the Nielsen–Ninomiya theorem about putting handed particles on a lattice;
  - the ways lattice physicists get around it.

**Honest caveat:** the handedness theorem is about the winding of a transport rule. How that connects to the weak force's handedness isn't established. Working that out is the first step.

## 5. Your Λ thought: "flow meets boundary" (C230, C235, D14)

**What the formula is.** (3/8π) H₀² M_P² is the **critical density**: the density a flat universe needs, about 8.5 × 10⁻²⁷ kg per cubic metre. Dark energy is 68.5% of it. The cosmological constant Λ itself is 3 × 0.685 × H₀², which is 2.85 × 10⁻¹²² in Planck units. The check `checks/critical_density.py` confirms all three.

**Why it looks meaningful, and the catch.**
- **H₀² M_P² is the only way to build a density from the expansion rate and gravity's strength,** so *any* idea tied to the expansion rate lands at about the right size. That's dimensional analysis, not evidence.
- **Your "flow meets boundary" reading does have a real published home:**
  - **Cohen, Kaplan and Nelson (1999)** argued that the vacuum energy in a region can't exceed what would make the region collapse into a black hole its own size. With the Hubble radius as the size, that gives this density (C225).
  - **Hsu (2004)** showed that using the Hubble radius makes dark energy behave like ordinary matter, which is ruled out (C226).
  - **Li (2004)** fixed that by using the *future event horizon*, the edge of what we can ever see (C157).
  - **That version currently fits DESI distances plus supernovae about as well as constant dark energy,** not better (Huang, Liu, Shao, 2026, C227).
- **Padmanabhan's "space emerges to balance horizon and bulk degrees of freedom" (C156) is the same spirit.**

**For ED:** "births set by the horizon" (C158) would **replace the tiny unexplained number with an order-1 number** and give slightly changing dark energy. It's testable right away with the distance pipeline we already built.

**Catch:** it relies on counting by horizon area, which competes with exact draws (C143).
