# Space that settles into 3D: options (B) and (C), and Allen's 3D ideas

*2026-09-13. G30 reopened. Ledger: C156–C168, D11. Follows [Locus_Birth.md](Locus_Birth.md).*

## 1. More births at the cosmic horizon? (Allen's G29 thought)

Allen suspected this "messes up a lot of stuff". That's right for one version, and there's a published version that doesn't.

**The problem: the cosmic horizon isn't a place.**
- **Every observer has their own horizon.** A locus at the edge of my view is in the middle of someone else's.
- **So "more births at the horizon" needs a special observer at the centre.** That is a preferred place, which ED rules out (C76).
- **A locus also can't tell, locally, that it's on someone's horizon.**
- **Every locus is on *some* observer's horizon,** so the fair version is back to the same birth chance everywhere, RD29 (C158).

**The version that works is published.**
- **Padmanabhan (2012, C156):** space "emerges" at a rate set by the difference between how many degrees of freedom sit on the horizon and how many sit inside it.
- **Li (2004, C157):** the size of dark energy is set by the event horizon.
- In both, the horizon sets **how fast space grows overall**, not **where** new pieces appear.

**What it could do for ED.** It could fix RD29's one free number: a birth chance set by a horizon count, with births still spread evenly in place.

**The catch** (C158). Counting by the horizon means loci obey a holographic count (degrees of freedom growing with horizon area). That same assumption is what makes exact draws fail (C143). So ED would probably have to choose between a horizon-set birth rate and exact draws.

## 2. Allen's 3D ideas

| Allen's idea | what's known | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **3D is the only dimension where a loop can really be a loop** (knotted or linked) | A closed loop can be knotted only in 3D. In 4D and up, every knot comes undone (C160). In 2D, loops can't link either | **Correct,** read as knotting and linking |
| **3D is the highest dimension where events stay ordered** | Tegmark (C159): with more than 3 space dimensions there are no stable atoms; with fewer than 3, no gravitational attraction; with more or fewer than 1 time dimension, nature isn't predictable | **Close to a known result,** read as stability and predictability |
| *(ED's own piece)* | With q = 1, the budget settles only in 3 or more dimensions (C84) | 3 is the **lowest** dimension that works for the budget |
| **Each force gets a dimension, in some sense** | Kaluza–Klein theories get forces from extra dimensions. Witten (1981, C161) found that getting the Standard Model's forces that way needs **7 extra hidden dimensions** (11 in total), and even then the particles don't come out right | **The idea exists, but not as "one of our three dimensions per force".** Kept as Allen's intuition (D11), not a claim |

**The pincer** (C162):
- 3 is the **lowest** dimension where ED's budget settles.
- 3 is the **highest** where knots and stable atoms exist.

It's a neat squeeze, but honestly it is two different kinds of argument: one from ED's rule, one from continuum physics. It is not a derivation.

**Link to G30.** Whatever makes space settle has to land on 3, and the pincer is a candidate for *why*. One caution: a loop in a graph isn't a knot. Knots need space to already be there to tie in.

## 3. (B) and (C) in plain words

### (B) A score for the whole shape

**Picture.**
- Every possible way of connecting all the loci gets a **score** for how space-like it is.
- Randomness proposes shapes. The score decides which ones count, and better-scoring shapes are far more likely.
- The score usually comes from gravity's equations. On graphs it is often counted from **short loops**: triangles and squares tell a graph how it is curved (C166).

**Track record.** Causal dynamical triangulations do this, adding a time layering built in by hand. A large-scale **4D world emerges** (C163, C153).

**What it would cost ED:**
- **It weighs whole histories against each other.** In effect it sums over many possible universes. ED's picture is **one** growing record with one-way steps (D1, D8).
- **The score comes with its own settings.**
- **The time layering is imposed,** not derived.

### (C) Links that let go and grab again

**Picture.**
- **Loci persist.** Links are like handshakes: a locus can drop one and grab another nearby.
- **Each locus follows a simple local preference,** such as "hold about this many hands" and "like being in short loops".
- **No blueprint is needed.** Over time the network settles into a shape, the way water freezes into a crystal. No molecule knows the plan, but local bonding preferences give order.

**Track record:**
- **Quantum graphity** (C164). When "hot", every locus is linked to every other and there is no geometry. As it "cools", the graph becomes ordered, low-dimensional and local. The best evidence found was a **hexagonal, 2D** pattern; whether 3D can form was left for future work.
- **Conrady** (C165). A local rule gives **2D surfaces** at low temperature, which dissolve when hot.
- **Akara-pipattana, Chotibut and Evnin** (C167). Fix the number of links and favour triangles or squares, and **cubes and pieces of lattice** appear, even though they were never programmed in.
- **Kelly, Trugenberger and Biancalana** (C166). Curvature is counted from short loops, and the best configurations are **cubic complexes**.

**Why it fits ED better than (B):**
- **One history, local steps,** like D1 and D8.
- **No score over whole universes.**
- **The preference is about loops,** which loosely echoes Allen's loop idea.

**What it would cost ED:**
1. **Links would not be permanent.** RD20 says loci carry their connections. Under (C), loci are the lasting record and links are space's current shape. That is Allen's call.
2. **The local preference is itself a choice:** how many links, and which loops.
3. **Settling into a perfect crystal would be bad.** A crystal has built-in directions, which means a preferred frame (T6, C48). The target is a shape that is 3D but disordered, more like glass than crystal.
4. **These models need something like temperature.** Geometry appears when "cool". ED has no temperature yet, and what would play that role is open.
5. **No (C)-type model has produced clean 3D yet.** The results so far are 2D surfaces, hexagons and cubic pieces (C168).

### How (B) and (C) relate

**The same preference can be used either way:**
- as a score for the whole shape, which is (B);
- as a rule each link follows over time, which is (C).

**(C) is the one-history, local version, which is why it suits ED.** Its literature is younger, and it hasn't delivered 3D.

## If (C): the first check

It would be a frozen check like the splitting test:
1. **Start** from loci with random links.
2. **Each step,** a random link lets go and re-attaches to a nearby locus (a neighbour's neighbour), if that improves the local preference:
   - (a) about 6 links per locus;
   - (b) more square loops.
3. **Measure** the random-walk dimension (d_s) and how count grows with size (D_g), against a 3D lattice.
4. **Predictions are written down before running.**

**Pass condition: 3D and glass-like, not a crystal.** The literature suggests lattice pieces or 2D are more likely than clean 3D.
