## What physics says (C13)

- **Bekenstein (1973):** a black hole's entropy is **the information hidden from outside:** an outside observer's lack of information about what's inside. Entropy measures missing information.
- **Jacobson and Parentani (2003):** the laws of horizon thermodynamics, and their statistical content, extend to **any causal horizon.** That includes the Rindler horizon of an accelerating observer, the boundary of everything that observer can ever receive signals from.
- **Rindler horizons and entanglement:** an accelerating observer can't receive signals from beyond their horizon. The vacuum is made of pairs straddling it, and **tracing over what's beyond is what makes the vacuum look warm** (the Unruh effect).
- **Jacobson's 1995 derivation uses exactly these local causal horizons.**

## The chain (C14)

1. **Entropy is missing information** (Bekenstein): the standard meaning of entropy, not an ED input.
2. **What's out of reach is what has left the reach of a future path** (A1 RD50), and that boundary is a causal horizon.
3. **The present state is all there is** (A4 D21). So the present's missing information is **the relations that are part of the present but reach beyond its reach:** one end reachable, the other end not.
4. **Only counts are real, and each relation carries no number** (A1 RD15, A4 D4). So missing information is **the count of relations crossing the reach boundary,** each counting the same.
5. **Part 2 showed that count grows like the boundary's area** (C8, C9).
6. **So Jacobson's input,** entropy ∝ area on local causal horizons, **follows from ED's reach, counted.**

**If steps 2 and 3 are just readings of what ED already decided, nothing new was added.** The list would be shorter by the area-entropy input.

**A consistency, not a result:** counting relations across the cosmic horizon should give about 10¹²², A/4 in Planck units. That's the capacity attempt 1 already gave ED's permanent horizon (A1 RD50). The coefficient is still inherited.
