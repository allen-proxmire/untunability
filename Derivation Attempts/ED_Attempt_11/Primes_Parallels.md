## One caution, from your own primes work

Your primes collection tested ED directly, as a **negative control**: an ED-style process "diffracts as a liquid" — no crystal peaks. The paper's conclusion: **"it takes divisibility to turn liquid into crystal. ED's relation to the primes is contrast, not support."** So the parallels above are about **how the two systems are organised**, not a claim that ED and the primes share a mechanism. **The primes get their order from arithmetic (divisibility); ED has no divisibility.**

## An idea for road L, from the comparison

**In the primes, order comes from a strict exclusion rule applied at every scale** (the wheels). **In ED, the crammed shapes win because nothing excludes them at large scale.** The rival theory's layer (road L) works like a wheel between slices: a slice can only be followed by slices that can be joined to it by a proper layer, and **the number of ways to build that layer is what weighs the slice.** It's the nearest thing ED could have to a wheel. That's the idea road L starts from.
