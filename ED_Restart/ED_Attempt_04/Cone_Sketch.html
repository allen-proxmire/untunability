Allen's point: pyramids work in every dimension, but rotation doesn't. A rotation happens in a plane, one per pair of axes, so d dimensions have d(d−1)/2 ways to rotate. That equals the number of axes only when d = 3. Only in three dimensions does every rotation have its own axis and every axis its own rotation. That's why spin and angular momentum can be arrows here.

In plain terms: with round cones, "fill without overlap" happens only in two dimensions. With pyramids it happens in every dimension: one double pyramid per axis gives a square's 4 sides in 2D, a cube's 6 faces in 3D, and a 4D cube's 8 faces in 4D. So pyramids do better, and the 45° becomes exact. But the picture then says "one per axis", not "only three fit": picking three still needs a reason.
