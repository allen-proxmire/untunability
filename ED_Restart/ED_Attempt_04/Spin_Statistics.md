# Road R2, part 2: why identical things exclude (spin–statistics)

*ED_Attempt_04, note 5. 2026-09-15 (RD5, RD6, D9). **Status: road R2 closed (RD6): consistent with known physics, not derived.** Ledger: C17–C19, C22, C23. Literature and reasoning; nothing computed.*

## Where R2 stands

**Allen decided a full locus is about *sameness*, not place** (D9). Two identical things can't be in the same state; different things can share a place. That matches physics, where exclusion belongs to identical fermions.

**The one specific result R2 could reach:** an ED reason for **why identical fermions exclude and bosons don't**. This note looks at how physics answers that.

## What physics knows

| result | what it says | ledger |
|---|---|---|
| **Fierz (1939), Pauli (1940): the spin–statistics theorem** | Particles with half-integer spin are fermions (they exclude); integer spin gives bosons (they share). The proof uses **relativity plus positive energy**, in quantum field theory | C17 |
| **No elementary explanation** | Physicists, Feynman among them, have long been dissatisfied that the proof needs complicated field-theory machinery and gives no physical insight | C17 |
| **Leinaas and Myrheim (1977): sameness plus space's shape** | For identical particles, swapping two isn't a new arrangement, so the space of arrangements has a twist. **In 3 or more dimensions, that twist allows only two kinds: bosons (+) and fermions (−).** In 1 or 2 dimensions a whole range in between is possible ("anyons") | C18 |
| **Berry and Robbins (1997): a geometric route** | Carry the spins along smoothly as two identical particles swap, and the fermion minus sign, (−1)^(2S), comes out automatically, without relativity. Whether it's a full proof is debated | C19 |

## What that means for ED (C22)

**Physics splits the answer into two parts:**
1. **Sameness plus three dimensions allows exactly two kinds** (Leinaas–Myrheim). Swapping identical things can multiply the state by only +1 or −1.
2. **Which particles get which sign is tied to spin:** half-integer spin gets −1. That link needs relativity (Pauli) or a transport rule (Berry–Robbins).

**Where ED has a hook for part 1:**
- **ED's draw fixes only records** (A1 RD50). Swap two identical things, and the committed world holds exactly the same record. So in ED, **"swapping identical things isn't a new state" could be a fact about records.** That fits Allen's sameness decision (D9) naturally.
- **But the "only two kinds" conclusion needs space to have three dimensions,** and ED doesn't derive that. Attempt 1 showed its gravity settles only in 3 or more dimensions, which rules out 1 and 2 but doesn't explain 3 (A1 C87).

**Where ED has nothing yet for part 2:**
- **ED has no spin.** Nothing in its rules says what spin is, or why half-integer spin should pick up the minus sign.

## What it means (C23)

- **With Allen's sameness decision, R2 is consistent with known physics.** Exclusion belongs to identical things in the same state.
- **An ED reason for *why* needs two things ED doesn't have:** three dimensions and spin.
- **So R2's specific result is out of reach for now.**
- **One real bridge, which connects to Allen's emergence questions (note 6):** *if* space emerges with three dimensions, then sameness automatically allows only two kinds of identical particle. Sameness would come first, and the dimension of space would decide how identical things can swap. That doesn't derive anything yet. It says where a derivation would have to come from.
