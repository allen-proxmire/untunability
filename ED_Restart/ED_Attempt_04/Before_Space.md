## What's known about space and time emerging (C20, C21)

| idea | what it says |
|---|---|
| **Quantum graphity** (Konopka, Markopoulou, Smolin 2006) | Start with a set of points **all connected to each other, the same way**: a "complete graph". There's no distance, no locality and no centre, and swapping any two points changes nothing. As the system cools, most connections switch off, leaving a low-dimensional lattice. **Space, and locality, emerge.** In the hot phase every point is next to every other. Problems with the picture are noted in later work |
| **Page and Wootters** (1983) | The universe as a whole can be **static and timeless**, yet parts of it see change, because one part serves as a clock for another. **Time emerges from correlations.** Illustrated with two entangled photons in 2013 |

## Allen's ball, in those terms

**A "ball without space and time" is, in the graphity picture, the complete graph:**
- **every locus related to every other, all in the same way;**
- **no distance, so no centre.** That removes the centre problem note 4 raised: there's no middle for ripples to come from.
- **complete sameness.** It's attempt 1's "one is none" (A1 D16) exactly. When every relation is identical, no relation says anything.

## Relation, gradient, boundary, before space and time (C24)

Allen's frame (A2 D1), read in order:

| | before the first difference | what the first difference brings |
|---|---|---|
| **Relation** | **Already there:** every locus related to every other, all identically. Full relation, zero information | Relations start to differ |
| **Gradient** | **None.** A gradient needs a difference across something | **The first gradient *is* the first difference:** some relations stronger or weaker than others. That's the moment Allen's ball "sees a difference" |
| **Boundary** | **None.** Everything is next to everything | **Where relations fall off, boundaries appear:** a locus's neighbours versus everything else. **That's locality, which is what space is** |

**So, read this way, the three come in sequence:**
- **relation exists first;**
- **a gradient breaks the sameness;**
- **boundaries turn the broken sameness into places.**

**Time** would come from the *order* of commitments: which difference was fixed before which. Commitment can't be undone, so that order has a direction. **Space** would come from the *pattern* of unequal relations: who is near whom.

## Sameness before place

- **Before the first difference, there are no places, only sameness and difference.** So a rule about **sameness** is the kind of rule that can exist *before* space; a rule about place can't.
- **Allen's decision to make a full locus about sameness (D9) fits this order.**
- **The bridge from note 5:** once space emerges, *if* it has three dimensions, sameness allows only two kinds of identical particle, bosons and fermions (Leinaas–Myrheim). Sameness first; then space's dimension decides how identical things can swap.

## Starting with commitment

**What might break the first sameness?** Attempt 1 already noted that an exactly uniform start never changes by itself: "the first contrast has to come from somewhere, and ED's chance at commitment is the natural source" (A1 C261).

So, in ED's own terms, **the first difference is the first draw:** the first commitment that makes some relation different from the rest. Everything after is commitments building on commitments:
- **their order is time;**
- **their pattern of nearness is space.**

## What pushes back

- **Known programs already do most of this.** Quantum graphity (space from a complete graph), Page–Wootters (time from correlations), and causal sets (spacetime from the order of events, already in attempt 1's reading) cover the ground. **For this to be ED's, ED has to add something they don't.** The candidates are *sameness first* (D9) and *the first draw as the first difference*.
- **Graphity has known problems.** The literature notes difficulties in getting a realistic universe out of it. Not read here.
- **Three dimensions still isn't derived.** Nothing here says why the lattice that emerges has three dimensions. Yet both the boson/fermion bridge and attempt 1's gravity need three (A1 C87).
- **Sync versus clumping** (note 4) is unresolved. How the universe "returns" toward sync while gravity clumps matter and spreads clock rates isn't addressed.
