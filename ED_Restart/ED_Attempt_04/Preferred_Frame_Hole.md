# The preferred-frame hole (on paper)

*ED_Attempt_04, note 15. 2026-09-15 (RD19, D30). Ledger: C84–C86. Literature and reasoning; nothing computed. **Meaning questions PF-Q1 and PF-Q2 and the draft exit rule are for Allen.***

## The hole

**Two open ends meet here:**
- **Gravity:** conservation fixed five of the ten gravity-test numbers (C83). It leaves **α1, α2 and ξ** open. They measure whether there's a preferred frame or place.
- **The speed part:** pairings give special relativity exactly, but only in one space dimension. **In three, a fixed grid picks out directions and a rest frame** (A1 C48; C78).

**The question:** can a discrete pattern of relations have **no rest frame**?

## What's known (C84)

| | what it says |
|---|---|
| **A regular grid** | Picks out directions and a rest frame. Standard |
| **Bombelli, Henson, Sorkin (2009), a theorem** | Points scattered at random in spacetime pick out no frame. **But no rule that gives each point a finite number of neighbours can do so without picking a frame** (A1 C48) |
| **Causal sets** (Dowker, Henson, Sorkin 2004) | Discreteness *can* be Lorentz invariant: a causal set's discreteness is locally frame-free. **The price: each element is linked to infinitely many others,** so locality becomes a problem (Sorkin 2007, "Does locality fail at intermediate length scales?") |
| **Hiding a frame at the Planck scale** (Collins, Perez, Sudarsky, Urrutia, Vucetich 2004) | With the known particle interactions, a Planck-scale preferred frame **leaks into everyday physics at the percent level,** about 20 orders of magnitude above naive estimates, unless the theory is **unnaturally fine-tuned**. Supersymmetry is one proposed softener |
| **Lorentz invariance emerging at low energy** | It can emerge along the flow to low energies (Chadha and Nielsen 1983). In strongly coupled holographic examples, deviations are power-law suppressed (Bednik, Pujolas, Sibiryakov 2013) |
| **Deformed relativity** | Discrete quantum walks recover special relativity only approximately, bent at the highest energies (Bisio, D'Ariano, Perinotti; C74) |

**The bounds any frame has to hide under:**
- **Light's speed doesn't depend on its energy:** no linear effect below about 6 Planck energies (LHAASO, GRB 221009A).
- **No vacuum birefringence:** the parameter is below about 10⁻¹⁵ to 10⁻¹⁶ (gamma-ray burst polarization).
- **Matter, light and gravity** are tested across the Standard-Model Extension data tables (Kostelecký and Russell).
- **Gravity:** α1 < 3.4 × 10⁻⁵, α2 < 1.6 × 10⁻⁹ (C79).

## Pick two of three (C85)

**Three things ED might want:**
1. **Discrete:** loci, only counts (A1 RD15, RD20).
2. **Finite neighbours:** each locus relates to finitely many others. Counting outward, E-Q2's way of reading dimension, needs this.
3. **No rest frame.**

**The theorem allows only two:**

| keep | give up | example | cost for ED |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 + 2** | no rest frame | a grid, or any finite-neighbour pattern | **ED has a rest frame.** It must hide it under the bounds, and faces the fine-tuning leak |
| **1 + 3** | finite neighbours | a causal set | **Counting outward breaks** (everything is a couple of links away). Dimension would need another reading, which reopens E-C's bridge S and E-Q2 |
| **2 + 3** | discreteness | a smooth continuum | Not ED |

**ED as decided sits at 1 + 2. So ED's substrate has a rest frame.**

## What a frame costs ED

- **It has to hide under every bound above.**
- **The fine-tuning leak is the real obstacle.** A frame at the smallest scale, plus ordinary particle interactions, tends to show up at the percent level. **ED names no mechanism that protects against that.** This is where a naive grid version of ED would fail, by about 20 orders of magnitude.

## What ED has that might help (candidates, not results)

| | candidate | what it gives | limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PF-a** | **The frame is relational:** the rest frame of the pattern itself. The universe has one anyway, the cosmic microwave background's rest frame | A frame that exists without being put in | Physics would still have to be blind to it. That's the same hole, relabelled |
| **PF-b** | **Frame-blind ticks.** The pairing count (D29) doesn't change under a boost. Scale right hops by D and left hops by 1/D: 4·(D·n_R)·(n_L/D) = 4·n_R·n_L | A body's own ticks don't see the frame, even if the hop pattern does | One dimension; counts only scale approximately; the pattern itself still has a frame |
| **PF-c** | **Random growth.** Draws make the pattern irregular, so there are no grid directions | Removes preferred *directions* on average | The theorem still gives a rest frame with finite neighbours |
| **PF-d** | **Give up finite neighbours.** A locus is related to everything in its past, like a causal set. D11 (time as order, space as pattern) is already close to causal sets (C26) | No rest frame, by the theorem's own example | Breaks E-Q2's counting and E-C's bridge; nonlocal |

## Honest note

**This may be the hardest wall for a discrete ED.** It's the known wall for every discrete approach except causal sets. Causal sets get past it by giving up finite neighbours. The hole doesn't close anything decided so far, but it's where attempt 1's grid and the speed part's one-dimensional result both stop.
