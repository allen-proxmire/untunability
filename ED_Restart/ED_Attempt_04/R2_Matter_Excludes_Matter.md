# Road R2: matter excludes matter

## The starting point

**Allen's decision D5:** two bodies can't be present at the same locus. A full locus is ordinary: matter excludes matter.

**The guard for this road:** rediscovering the known physics of exclusion isn't a result. It only counts if something specific comes out that physics doesn't already supply.

## What physics knows about exclusion (C10, C11)

| fact | what it says |
|---|---|
| **Pauli exclusion** | Two **identical fermions** (electrons, protons, neutrons) can't be in the **same quantum state**. It's about *states*, not *places* |
| **Bosons share freely** | Bosons can pile into the same state and the same place. In a **Bose–Einstein condensate** many atoms occupy one state, their waves overlapping. Lasers are photons sharing one state |
| **Matter is stable because of Pauli** | Dyson and Lenard (1967), then Lieb and Thirring (1975), proved ordinary matter doesn't collapse. The proof needs at least one sign of charge to be fermions. **Made only of bosons, matter would collapse** |
| **Solidity and dead stars** | Pauli plus electrical forces set how big atoms are and why solids resist squeezing. The same "degeneracy pressure" holds up white dwarfs against gravity |

## Poking at "a full locus" (C15)

**If D5 means "no two bodies at the same place", ever:**
- **Bosons conflict.** Atoms in a condensate share place and state. A place rule forbids that. Saying massless things have no presence (A1 RD17: light has no ticking) doesn't help, because condensate atoms have mass.
- **Different particles overlap.** Electrons spend part of their time inside the atomic nucleus, and neutrinos pass through the Earth. A place rule between *different* particles forbids that.
- **The only escape is sparse presence.** If a particle is present on only a tiny fraction of the loci it spans, overlaps would almost never share a locus. **But then the exclusion almost never acts,** and can't explain solidity.
- **It can't give atoms their size.** Atomic size comes from Planck's constant, the electron's mass and its charge. An exclusion rule at the scale of loci carries none of those.

**Where physics puts exclusion: identical fermions in the same state.** That's a rule about *sameness*, not *place*.

**An association (flagged, not a finding).** Allen's earlier "one is none; identical things give poor relations, contrast gives rich ones" (A1 D16) resembles this: identical fermions exclude each other, and different things mix freely. It's worth remembering and not worth leaning on.

## What it means so far (C16)

- **So R2 is first a meaning question: is a full locus about *where* or about *sameness*?**
- **The only specific result R2 could reach is an ED reason for why identical fermions exclude.** That would need its own literature step on the spin–statistics theorem first.
