# Annihilation on paper

*ED_Attempt_04, note 17. 2026-09-15 (D34). Ledger: C90–C92. Literature and reasoning; nothing computed. **The exit rule was confirmed by Allen (D34):** if uncuttable knots can't account for annihilation without cutting, record the conflict, and carry D14 into attempt 5 as needing revision.*

## The question

**What ED decided:**
- **A particle is a knot of commitments** that can't be cut, and whose strands can't pass through each other (D14, D16).
- **Commitments never stop** (E-Q3, D24).

**What happens every day:**
- **An electron and a positron annihilate** into two gamma rays of 511 keV each, flying off back to back. **PET scanners detect exactly these pairs** (C90).
- **The reverse, pair creation, happens too.**

**So: can ED's particle allow annihilation?**

## Two separate problems (C91)

### Problem A: kind

- **To annihilate, a particle and its antiparticle have to end up as "no particle":** a plain loop, or nothing.
- **Reshaping without cutting keeps a knot's type.** Joining two knots together never gives a plain loop, because a knot's genus adds when knots are joined (Schubert).
- **So under reshaping, knots have no inverses.** There's no "anti-knot" that cancels a knot, the way −1 cancels +1. Mathematicians call this structure a semigroup: it has a unit (the plain loop) but no inverses.
- **So a knotted particle can never be reshaped away,** alone or together with anything else. **That conflicts with annihilation.**

### Problem B: commitments

- **Even an unknotted particle can't vanish.** Vanishing removes its commitments, but commitments never stop (E-Q3).
- **Turning them into light doesn't help,** because light is uncommitted (D27), so that would be uncommitting.
- **This conflict doesn't depend on knots at all.**

## What physics does (C90)

| | what happens | why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Topological solitons** (skyrmions, hopfions) | They **do** annihilate with their antisolitons, in magnets and in field theory | Their charge is an **integer** (a winding number or Hopf number) that adds. Opposite charges sum to zero, so the pair can smoothly become nothing. **Integers have inverses** |
| **Knots in four-dimensional spacetime** | A knot joined to its reversed mirror image **can** be undone over time: it's a "ribbon" knot, which bounds a disc in four dimensions | **Only through saddle moves:** bands that cut two strands and rejoin them. In that setting (knot concordance) the reversed mirror image *is* the inverse |

**The lesson:**
- **Kinds that last *and* can annihilate need a charge with inverses.**
- **Knot type without cutting has none.**
- **It gets inverses only if cutting and rejoining in pairs is allowed** (concordance), **or if the charge is a field's winding** rather than a loop's knot type.
