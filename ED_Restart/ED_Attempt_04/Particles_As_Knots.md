## Particles as knots: the history (C36–C38)

| episode | what happened | ledger |
|---|---|---|
| **Kelvin's vortex atoms (1867)** | Atoms as knotted vortex tubes in the ether. Different knots were different elements; knots don't come undone under small nudges, which explained why atoms are stable; each knot vibrates its own way, which explained each element's colours of light. Tait built the first knot tables to catalogue them | C36 |
| **Why it died** | Almost all knotted vortex shapes turned out to be **dynamically unstable**, and the ether was undermined (Michelson–Morley). Gone before 1900. **Its lasting gift was knot theory itself** | C36 |
| **Knotted particles in modern field theory** | Faddeev and Niemi (1997): certain field theories have **stable knot-shaped solitons ("hopfions")**. Their knot number is a conserved charge and survives collisions, like a particle's identity. Related knotted structures are studied in magnets and superfluids. Skyrme's older model treats protons and neutrons as twisted field configurations | C37 |
| **Real knots in water (2013)** | Kleckner and Irvine made knotted vortices in water for the first time. **The knots stretched and untied themselves by reconnecting, i.e. by being cut.** Some vortex knots are topologically protected; in real fluids, reconnection lets knots escape | C38 |

## What that means for ED's knot idea (C40)

**Kelvin failed on two things ED would need to avoid:**
- **Stability.** His knots came undone. ED's version stands or falls on **"commitments can't be cut"**, the one thing that fails for real vortices, which reconnect. **So in ED that isn't a detail. It's the whole claim, and it's a meaning, not an observation.**
- **A medium.** Kelvin needed an ether. ED's knots would be made of relations, not of stuff in a medium. That's a real difference.

**Hopfions show that stable knotted particles are possible in principle.** Topology can protect them with a conserved "knot charge". In field theory, though, that protection comes from the equations' structure, not from "no cutting" as a rule.

**The trap Kelvin's program also shows: matching knot tables to particle tables is tempting, and easy to overdo.** If ED ever pairs specific knots with specific particles, that pairing has to be fixed before looking.

**So R5-Q1 is a genuine meaning question.** Allen would be deciding that commitment-knots can't be cut *by what a commitment is*. Physics doesn't supply that; it's what would make the idea ED's own.

## Allen's idea: forces set the dimension (D13)

> "if you imagine an x, y, z grid ... i imagine putting the forces in that and only 3 fit without 'overlap'. like its geometry. ... if you had 4 forces, they would somehow cancel or overlap. so we get 3."

**What the literature says (C39):**
- **The best-known link between forces and dimensions runs the other way.** In Kaluza–Klein theory, gravity in five dimensions looks like gravity *plus electromagnetism* in four: the extra force comes from an **extra, tiny, curled-up dimension**.
- **More forces need more hidden dimensions.** Getting the Standard Model's forces that way takes several more. So in known physics, forces don't live in the x, y, z grid; they live in extra, internal directions.

**Where the idea does touch real mathematics (C41):**
- **Three perpendicular axes go with exactly three independent turning motions.** One turn per pair of axes gives three in 3D, but six in 4D.
- **The weak force's symmetry group (SU(2)) has exactly three generators,** the same algebra as rotations in 3D. That's standard mathematics, and an association worth noting, not a finding.
- **Ehrenfest (note 7) ties the *shape* of forces to dimension.** The inverse-square law is what three dimensions give, and stable orbits need it.

**What pushes back:**
- **There are four forces, counting gravity,** and gravity isn't a force in the grid at all. In general relativity it *is* the geometry.
- **The strong force's group has eight generators, not three.** "Three forces, three axes" doesn't match the internal structure of the forces themselves.
- **"Overlap" needs a rule.** The most natural reading, "each force needs its own independent direction", makes dimension just a count of forces. That's circular unless something says why forces need separate directions.

**A reading that could be ED's own (untested):**
- **The rule would be:** dimension equals **the number of independent kinds of relation** that can coexist without interfering.
- **It fits Allen's "relations among different things are richer"** (A1 D16): different kinds need room to differ.
- **It fits his timing too:** dimension would be set by the rules of relation, *before or with* space, not after.
- **The poke:** it would still need a reason for *three* kinds. Counting ED's three channels, or the three gauge forces, and calling it three dimensions is the kind of match the ledger treats as a hint (A2 D5), not evidence.

## What it means (C42)

- **Particles as knots is an old idea with a mixed record.** It failed as vortex atoms, where the knots weren't stable. It works in modern field theory, where topology protects them. It fails in real fluids, where knots get cut.
- **ED's version is only different if "commitments can't be cut" is part of what a commitment *is*.** That makes R5-Q1 a meaning decision for Allen, now informed by that history.
- **Forces set the dimension:**
  - **Known physics:** forces come *with* extra dimensions, not by filling ordinary ones.
  - **The math it does touch:** three axes, three rotations, and the weak force's three generators.
  - **A possible ED reading:** dimension as the number of independent kinds of relation. It would need its own reason for three.
