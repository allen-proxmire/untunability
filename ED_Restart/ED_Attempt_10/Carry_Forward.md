## The question attempt 9 answered

**Is the kind of growth rule right?** No — it's missing one ingredient. **Nothing in ED's growth prefers an extended shape.** Random change drifts toward crammed small worlds because there are vastly more of them, and beating that needs a strong preference that grows with the size of the slice. ED doesn't have one.

## What's measured and known

- **Flat space turns into a small world under any random change, however rare, within a few dozen ticks, and never turns back** (A9 C37).

## Lessons

- **Calibrate every reading on shapes with a known answer first**, and let the calibration stop the road.
- **Fix pass rules before the data** — and make them test *build-up*, not an end-point a one-off bump can satisfy (A9 C31).
- **Judge the spacetime**, as attempt 7 decided; attempts 8 and 9 drifted back to slices.
- **Start from a messy state as well as a flat one** — a flat start that stays flat may only mean nothing happened (A9 C35).

## The opening road

| | road | the question |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | **What sets the shape?** | Something must prefer extended space, strongly, and more so as the slice grows. Where can ED get that? |

**Two parts:**

- **S1 — on paper: is there something ED conserves that would do it by itself**, the way the budget sets size? This is the ED-shaped hope. If found, it would be the most important result this project could produce. No design exists yet.
- **S2 — the test: tune a strength**, as CDT does, in a proper weighing over whole histories. It tells us whether *any* strength gives three-dimensional space. Days to build; the strength would be a tuned setting.

**S1 first.** It costs nothing but thought, and it decides what S2 should test.

**Also carried, not opened:** amplitudes (weights that can cancel); road R (a proper test of rates matching across the whole pattern); road I (interactions need clocks within range — the Pound–Rebka idea); road D (does ED's balance need genuine chance?).
