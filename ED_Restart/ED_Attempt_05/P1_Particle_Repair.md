## The conflict being repaired (A4 C91, C92)

**Attempt 4 decided:**
- **a particle is a knot of commitments that can't be cut** (A4 D14);
- **its strands can't pass through each other** (A4 D16);
- **commitments never stop** (A4 D24).

**But particles and antiparticles annihilate.** An electron and a positron become two gamma rays, which is what PET scans detect. That creates two conflicts:
- **Kind:** under reshaping alone, no knot joined to another ever gives a plain loop, so a knotted particle can't annihilate.
- **Commitments:** a vanishing particle would end commitments, and they "never stop".

**The leads attempt 4 left:**
- **L1:** cutting and rejoining in pairs;
- **L2:** kinds as field charges;
- **L3:** "never stop" read as "what happened stays happened".

## What's known (C26)

| | fact | source |
|---|---|---|
| **Cancelling knots** | A knot joined to its **mirror image with its direction reversed** (written −K) bounds a disc in four dimensions ("slice"). **In the "concordance" way of counting knots, −K is K's inverse** | Knot concordance (Ray; Teichner; A4 C90) |
| **How many kinds survive** | In three dimensions, knots up to concordance form a **huge group**: infinitely many independent kinds, some of order 2. Circles don't knot at all in four or more dimensions, so there's only one kind there | Levine; Casson and Gordon; Jiang; Zeeman (A4 C68) |
| **The catch** | **Cutting and rejoining in pairs, with no restriction, can untie any knot.** A crossing change is a pair of saddles (a fission then a fusion), a history with one handle. Only histories **without handles** (genus 0) keep the concordance kind | Slice genus and cobordism results |
| **Antiparticles** | **Feynman and Stückelberg:** an antiparticle is a particle running backward in time. Physics is invariant under the combined C, P and T operations | Feynman–Stückelberg; the CPT theorem |
| **Minimal lengths** | Proposals for a shortest length differ in kind: lattices, modified uncertainty relations, causal sets, and more. **Discreteness doesn't have to mean a grid** | Hossenfelder, *Living Rev. Relativ.* 16, 2 (2013) |

## The repair (C27)

**Put three pieces together:**
1. **L3, "what happened stays happened"** (P-Q1). A commitment can end; its having happened stays reflected in the present. That's already how D21 reads: no records, just the present.
2. **L1, cuts and rejoins only in pairs** (P-Q2). Two strands swap partners at one place, and no strand ever just dangles.
3. **Kinds kept up to histories without handles** (P-Q3): the concordance kind.

**What it gives:**
- **Annihilation works.** A particle K meets its antiparticle −K. Joined, they form a slice knot, and a handle-free history shrinks it to nothing.
  - **The commitments end** (P-Q1), and what's left is uncommitted pattern: light (A4 D27), carrying the motion and energy (A4 D30).
  - **Two gamma rays.** Pair creation runs the same history backward.
- **Kinds are still conserved.** A kind can only disappear by meeting its inverse.
  - **So unlike particles can't annihilate completely:** K joined to −K′ isn't slice unless K and K′ are the same kind.
  - **That matches physics:** an electron doesn't annihilate completely with an antimuon.
- **E-C survives:**

| dimensions | kinds (up to concordance) |
|---|---|
| 2 | loops can't knot: one kind |
| **3** | **many** (as many as the loci allow) |
| 4 or more | circles don't knot: one kind |

**More than one kind still happens only in 3D.**

## The honest costs (C28)

- **The handle-free rule is chosen,** because without it paired cuts would untie every knot and nothing would last. **It has a fitting risk,** and the note labels it that way. A meaning-based case for it:
  - "a relation can't form a closed loop and remove it again inside one history";
  - but that reading isn't forced.
- **CPT isn't derived.** The reversed mirror is P plus T in knot terms, and charge conjugation isn't addressed.
- **Associations, logged as hints, not results:**
  - **The antiparticle as a reversed mirror** fits Feynman–Stückelberg, and it fits the old ED repo's "matter read against the grain of time" (A4 C28).
  - **Knots that equal their own reversed mirror** (order 2, like the figure-eight knot) would be **their own antiparticle,** as some neutral particles are.
- **Spin, charge and which knot is which particle** are still not reached.
- **The small-scale dimension** (A4 H1) and **the rest frame** are unchanged.

## Allen's grain question (D9, C29)

> "EDs grid is supposed to be relational, scaleless, but in the committed world it relies on the Planck length... idk really know how to say what the grain is."

**What attempt 5 already says about it:**
- **Only counts are real** (A1 RD15). A pattern of relations has no metres in it. **Scale only appears when counts are compared with physical units.**
- **Road J changed what the Planck length is in ED** (C10):
  - it's **how densely relations cross a surface:** ℓ_P² ≈ ℓ_g²/(k·s₀);
  - **so the Planck length isn't the size of a cell.** It's a ratio of counts: crossings per unit of area.
- **Discrete doesn't have to mean a grid:**
  - **causal sets are discrete with no grid** (A4 C84);
  - **the dispersion check found grids in trouble** (C24, C25): one-tick grid rules slow short waves, and simple ones pick out directions;
  - **a direction-free random pattern** avoids both, and it's what road J's area law needed anyway (S′).
- **So "we end up on it" doesn't have to happen.** ED can be discrete, counts only, with no rigid grid. The Planck length then shows up as a crossing density, not as a tile size.
- **What's still open:** a random pattern still has a rest frame (A4 C87), and how waves travel on it hasn't been worked out.
