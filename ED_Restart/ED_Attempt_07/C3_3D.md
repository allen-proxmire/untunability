## Where C3a left it (C53)

> **"Growing a flat 2D slice isn't reached with ED's local moves; the quadratic curvature cost flattens locally but not at large scales."**

**The lesson:** a local cost acts only locally. Keeping a slice smooth at large scales needs something that **carries large-scale information into a local decision.** In 2D, sync is borderline and can't do it (C2b). The question here is whether it can in 3D.

## The question

> **Can a 3D slice grow without splitting and stay smooth, with commitment, curvature cost and sync each doing a job ED's meanings already give them?**

## On paper

### 1. The moves (C55)

**As in 2D, a slice is a triangulation of fixed topology** (a 3-torus), and "never splits or merges" means the topology never changes (C3-Q1).
- **A childless event:** edge contraction under the link condition (Dey–Edelsbrunner). That's the exact 3D form of C3a's collapse.
- **Extra children:** vertex split, the inverse. The 3D choice is bigger: a split cuts the sphere of a vertex's neighbours into two discs, instead of cutting a ring into two arcs.
- **Rewiring without changing the count:** 2–3 and 3–2 flips. These are ED's "cut and rejoin" moves (A6 D5, D8).
  - **A flag:** a 2–3 move adds one link and a 3–2 move removes one, so they're not "in pairs" in the strict sense. A6's "only in pairs" would need to be read as cut-and-rejoin, not equal numbers.
- **Budget:** the same as C3a (C48). Size is handled.

### 2. Three bad shapes, three ED meanings (C56)

**C45 named two bad shapes; the literature adds a third,** hyperbolic small worlds (Bianconi–Rahmede; A6's own small worlds). Each one breaks a different meaning ED already has:

| bad shape | what it looks like | the ED meaning that rules it out | how |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Crumpled** | Everything near everything; events with huge numbers of neighbours | **Commitment** (each link costs) and **no infinities** | The cost grows with degree; a finite ceiling on neighbours |
| **Hyperbolic small world** | Bounded degree, but volume grows exponentially; negatively curved everywhere | **Quadratic energy** (curvature cost) | In 3D, curvature sits on edges: an edge shared by more tetrahedra than the flat value (**2π/arccos(1/3) ≈ 5.10**) is negatively curved. A quadratic cost in (edge degree − 5.10) penalizes it |
| **Branched polymer** | Tree-like: big regions hanging off thin necks; spectral dimension 4/3 | **Sync** | Next section: a thin neck can't hold a synced now for a large region behind it |
| *What's left* | **Flat, three-dimensional** | — | Where CDT finds its good phase |

**C3a's lesson applies to the second row.** A curvature cost alone flattened a surface locally, but not at large scales. So the third row, sync, is the one that has to supply large-scale information.

### 3. How sync acts at large scales (C57)

**The tick-count field is a large-scale object read locally.**
- Each event's tick count is set by its neighbours, theirs by their neighbours, and so on across the whole slice. It's the steady state of C2b's update.
- **So the tick difference across a single link carries information about the whole region behind it,** like a potential.

**At a thin neck:**
- **Rate surplus:** a region of n events hanging off a neck of m links has a random rate surplus of about σ√n (persistent rates, C2-Q5).
- **Pull:** the neck can pull at most about K·m (bounded pull, G-Q8).
- **Locking:** the region stays locked only if **m ≳ (σ/K)·√n.**

**How the neck compares with the region, by slice shape:**

| slice | neck size m for a region of n events | compared with √n | sync pressure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3D flat** | about n^(2/3) (a ball's surface) | Much larger | **None:** necks are wide enough |
| **2D flat** | about n^(1/2) | **The same scaling:** set by σ/K | **Borderline** (why C3a found no large-scale push) |
| **Branched** | about 1 (a thin neck) | Much smaller | **Grows with the branch:** the bigger the branch, the more its "now" tears at the neck |

**So a growth rule that favours moves reducing the tick difference across links** would widen exactly the necks that matter:
- the pressure is strongest where a large region hangs off a thin neck;
- it's absent in a flat 3D slice.

That's a large-scale restoring force built entirely from local readings. It's the causal, time-ordered form of A6's "relations added where rates fail to match" (G-Q11). That version, without time order, headed to the floor; here the floor itself (3D) is where the pressure switches off.

### 4. The honest limits (C58)

| | limit |
|---|---|
| **The middle may be narrow** | In equilibrium 3D triangulations the crumpled and branched phases meet at a first-order transition, with no smooth phase between. CDT finds one only with a time slicing. ED has time order, but whether the three meanings open a real middle is **not known** |
| **Balance between forces** | Commitment pushes toward few links, which is branched; sync pushes against necks; curvature cost pushes toward the flat edge value. **Their relative strengths are knobs** (census guard) |
| **The curvature cost may only act locally** | As in 2D (C53). The argument relies on sync for large scales |
| **Whole-number dimension** | Still assumed for "three," though a flat middle would be three by construction of the moves |
| **Cost** | 3D slices large enough to read (about 30³ events) with Python moves could take many hours per run. A cheaper check comes first |

## What it means (C59)

- **In 3D, ED's three existing meanings line up with random geometry's three bad shapes, one each:**
  - commitment against crumpling;
  - quadratic energy against hyperbolic;
  - sync against branching.
- **Sync is the only one that acts at large scales.** It does so through the tick-count field. The pressure is zero in a flat 3D slice, borderline in 2D (C3a's result), and grows across thin necks.
- **Consistent, not derived.** A lead on paper; nothing computed. Inputs unchanged (3).

**Next step: C3b, a static check, specified on paper, then run.**

**The stand-in slices, each at several sizes:**
- a **flat 3D grid;**
- a **branched** slice (small 3D blocks joined in a random tree by thin necks);
- a **hyperbolic** slice (a bounded-degree graph with exponential volume growth);
- a **crumpled** slice (a random graph whose degrees grow with size).

**The signals:**
- **sync:** C2b's tick-count wobble and its largest tilt across any single link, against size;
- **commitment:** links per event;
- **curvature:** the volume growth exponent. A graph proxy, since only the flat stand-in is a triangulation.

**Recording:**
- **Pass:** each bad shape is flagged by its meaning and the flat slice by none. Sync tilt grows with size only on the branched slice, link cost grows only on the crumpled slice, and volume grows exponentially only on the hyperbolic slice.
