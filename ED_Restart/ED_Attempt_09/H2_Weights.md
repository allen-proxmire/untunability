## What H2 was going to ask

H1 left ED's slices **three-dimensional up close but riddled with shortcuts** — small worlds (C14, C15). H2 was going to ask whether ED's growth should use **weights that can cancel** (amplitudes, the Born rule's kind of number), on the reasoning that cancellation is what beats "there are far more bad shapes than good ones".

**The literature check says that reasoning was wrong, and I need to correct it first.**

## A correction to my own earlier claim (C16)

In attempt 8 (A8 C41) I wrote that **cancellation is what beats "more numerous"**, and that every approach which gets smooth spacetime relies on it. **That was overstated.**

**Causal dynamical triangulations — the closest approach that actually works — does not get its geometry from cancellation in practice.** It rotates its sum into one with **ordinary positive weights** (a "Wick rotation") and then samples it by Monte Carlo. The smooth, four-dimensional universe appears in that **positive-weight** version. What makes it work, according to its own reviews, is that **the action and the entropy of shapes are set to balance each other** in a particular range of its couplings.

So positive weights *can* give extended geometry. The thing ED's growth lacks is not obviously the minus signs.

**What CDT has that ED's growth doesn't, stated carefully:**

| | CDT | ED's growth |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The causal slicing** | slices never split or merge | **has it** |
| **2. How it's weighed** | **whole histories at once**, sampled until they settle | **grown forward one step at a time** |
| **3. What sets the size** | a coupling, **tuned** | a budget, **conserved** — Budgeted Causality |
| **4. What sets the shape** | further couplings, set to a range where action balances entropy | **nothing**, now the costs are gone |
| **5. What's judged** | **the whole spacetime** | mostly the slices |

## So H2 splits in two (C17)

**H2a — weights that can cancel.** Amplitudes instead of probabilities, so ED's own Born rule does work in growth. **No working example gets geometry this way**; it would be new territory. A large meanings decision, and it touches one of ED's three inputs.

**H2b — whole histories weighed together, instead of one history grown forward.** This is **CDT's actual route**. And your own decisions may already allow it:

> *"Once something has happened it's fixed, but many could have happened."* (D11 of attempt 8)
>
> *"The history isn't recorded anywhere or known to the present... you can undo history, but you can't really know what it was."* (D12 of attempt 8)

If many pasts were possible and the present doesn't record which one happened, then **weighing whole histories against each other is not a step away from ED — it may be what ED already says.** Growing one history forward and keeping it is a *modelling* choice, made because it's how the code was first written. It's the same pattern as sync and the costs: a modelling convenience that nobody decided was the meaning.

**H2b is the smaller change, has a working precedent, and may already follow from your decisions. H2a is the larger change with none.**

## And something already decided that drifted (C18)

**Attempt 7 decided (A7 D40) that the grown spacetime is what gets judged, not the slice alone** — because CDT's own spatial slices are **fractal**, with spectral dimension about **1.5**. Only CDT's spacetime is extended.

**Attempts 8 and 9 drifted back to judging slices.** Road E, road F and H1 all measured slices; the spacetime reading was cut for memory at the largest size.

**By that standard, H1's slices — spectral dimension 2.8–3.0 — are already better than CDT's own slices.** Their shortcuts matter, but **whether they spoil the spacetime has never been measured.** It's cheap at the two smaller sizes.

## What would stop the shortcuts (C19)

**In CDT, shortcuts are what the bad phases look like** — in the collapsed phase a few points connect to almost everything. **Being in the good phase is what keeps them out**, and the good phase comes from the balance in point 4 above.

**ED conserves what CDT tunes for size, but has no counterpart for what CDT uses to set the shape.** One of those CDT couplings concerns **how neighbouring slices connect in time** — again pointing at spacetime, not slices.

**So the honest statement is:** H1 removed what was pushing the wrong way. **What would push the right way isn't in ED yet.** H2b says where CDT gets it — whole histories weighed together — and D11–D12 suggest ED may allow it.
