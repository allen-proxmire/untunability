# Idea: space is the record

*Allen, 2026-09-13. Not adopted yet. Ledger: D8 (proposed), G28, C69–C73.*

## The idea

What if space is the record, the history? Space would emerge from events, and **not disappear**: it stays, with properties.

**What it would help with (Allen):**
- **Sparse commitment and the preferred frame.** Commitment has to be sparse on the scale of the universe for any preferred frame to be very small.
- **Voids.** If space only exists where things are happening, the cosmic voids would have almost no space, which always sat oddly. If space is what's left behind by events, voids have space because things happened there before.

---

## First: it conflicts with D1 as worded

D1 says a commitment is *"not a record kept anywhere"* and *"loci keep no memory."* This idea says space *is* a record. Both can't stand as written.

**What D1 was protecting.** The literature gates showed that if loci remembered past traffic and that memory biased what happens next (like ant scent), the outcome would be decided by settings ED doesn't fix (C18, C24, C33). D1 kept ED out of that trap.

**So the question is whether the record idea brings the trap back.** It depends on *what* is recorded.

## G28 — how does "space is the record" fit with D1? — needs Allen

**Claude's suggestion: (a).** It keeps what D1 was for, and gains what Allen is after.

## What (a) would buy, honestly

### Space in voids
Space would exist wherever events *have* happened, not only where they happen now. The early universe was busy and very uniform everywhere, so it would have left a nearly uniform record. That is a plausible story for why voids have space. **To count as more than a story,** the record would have to come out as uniform as the universe is measured to be.

### The preferred frame (T6)
This could be the biggest gain.
- **A rigid lattice has built-in directions.** A record built up by random, one-way commitments, each coming after earlier ones, is instead like a causal set.
- **Causal sets built that way pick out no preferred frame** (Bombelli, Henson and Sorkin 2009, C48), provided the record is an ordering of events ("this came after that"), not a fixed grid of neighbours.
- **So** "space is the record of sparse, random commitments" is close to *exactly* the structure known to avoid the preferred-frame problem.

### A growing universe
If every event adds to the record, space grows as things happen. Causal set cosmology has a published version of this idea: the number of spacetime elements grows, and random fluctuations in that number give a cosmological "constant" that keeps coming out about the size of the current matter density (Everpresent Λ, C71). This is suggestive for ED, not a result.

---

## What it would cost

- **ED moves very close to two existing programs** (C69, C70):
  - **Causal set theory:** spacetime is a growing, one-way record of events, grown by random sequential steps (Rideout and Sorkin 1999).
  - **Ellis's evolving block universe:** spacetime grows as the present moves forward, with the past fixed and the future open.
  
  What stays ED's own is what runs *on* the record: chains, draws, the budget, clock ticks.
- **One difference to take seriously** (C73). In causal set theory the recorded elements are atoms of spacetime itself, laid down everywhere regardless of matter. In ED they would be *commitments*. So the amount of space would track how much commitment happened in the past. That is a real difference, and it could be tested, or it could be ruled out.
- **The graph stops being a fixed lattice** (G1). The 1D ring still works as a test bed for version 0: it would be a small patch of space that has already been recorded.
- **"Remains with properties" needs care.** Every property the record keeps is a possible memory. Under (a) the least it could keep is order and connection. The per-locus budget is the natural property to attach, since it is already a property of places.
- **A new question.** Do clock ticks add to the record, or only draws?

## Round 2 (2026-09-13): loci come into being with space

**Allen.** He was thinking of it universally. In the beginning there aren't even loci, outside the great uniformity. The first difference was the first locus. Loci come into being as the universe inflates.

**Does that sidestep the issues? Partly.** Here is each one.

| issue | sidestepped? | why |
|---|---|---|
| **Voids have no space** | **Yes** | Loci born early, everywhere, persist. Voids formed later, inside space that already existed. |
| **The memory trap (D1)** | **Not by itself** | It depends on what a newborn locus carries. Allen's picture fits option (a) naturally: loci are *structure* born with space, carrying a budget, not traffic memory. |
| **Preferred frame (T6)** | **Only under two conditions** | (i) Loci are born at random and attach by "came after" relations, like causal set growth (C69), not laid out as a grid. (ii) Physics doesn't depend on the *order* the loci were born in. Causal set theory builds this in as "discrete general covariance" (C69). If physics could read the birth order, that order would itself be a preferred time. |
| **The first difference** | **Yes, and it joins up with way forward 1** | The first locus *is* the first difference. The fork still applies: it came from chance, a lopsided rule, or a non-uniform start. ED's commitments point to chance. |

**A close relative in the literature: quantum graphity** (C74). It starts from a graph whose points are all connected and all equivalent: no locality, a kind of great uniformity. In a low-energy phase, ordered, low-dimensional, local space emerges. Causal set sequential growth (C69) is the other relative: elements are born one at a time.

### G29 — are loci still being born today? — needs Allen
- **If only during inflation,** today's expansion needs another explanation.
- **If still ongoing,** what makes a new locus (commitments?), and how often? This links to Everpresent Λ (C71), where the growing count of elements sets the size of the cosmological constant.

### G30 — how does a new locus attach? — later
The birth rule. It must be random and one-way, and physics must not depend on birth order (the preferred-frame conditions above).
