# G34 unpacked: what does "heavier" mean, and a smallest unit of what?

*2026-09-13. Ledger: C93–C97. Replaces the withdrawn comparison in [Free_Numbers.md](Free_Numbers.md).*

**Allen's instinct:** "heavier pattern" doesn't land, and something seems to mix two scales. **He's right.** At least four separate things were hiding in that phrase. Unpacking them changes the answer.

---

## Scale 1: one part, or many hinged parts?

- **An electron is one part.** An atom or a C60 molecule is many parts hinged together (RD10).
- **If "too thin" applies to each part separately,** then when a molecule spreads, all its parts spread together and each reaches its own limit at about the same point. Having more parts doesn't let it spread further.
- **If it applies to the whole pattern's total,** more parts would let it spread further.

**Proposal G34a:** the limit applies to each part separately, since each hinged part is its own pattern.

## Scale 2: total amount, or the ticking share?

**The split.** RD17 says a pattern's commitment splits between moving and ticking, and the split has to be Pythagorean (C63). In standard physics that is exactly the energy relation E² = (pc)² + (mc²)² (C95). Total energy splits into a moving share (momentum) and a resting share (mass).

**So "amount" could mean two different things:**

| meaning | a moving electron | light |
|---|---|---|
| **Total amount** (energy) | Grows with speed | Has it |
| **Ticking share** (rest mass) | Stays the same | Has none |

If the limit used rest mass, light would have none and would be "too thin" instantly. Yet light stays spread for hundreds of kilometres (T10, C92). If it used total energy, it runs into Scale 4.

## Scale 3: how far, or how fast?

- **How far** a pattern may spread before a forced draw is a count of loci.
- **How fast** it spreads is set by ħ/m (C44). A part with more rest mass spreads more slowly, so it takes longer to reach the same limit.
- **Károlyházy and Diósi talk about a third thing** (C64): how far apart two branches of a *whole body* can be, and how quickly that superposition collapses, depending on the body's total mass and size.

So "heavier things stay coherent over shorter distances" in those models is about composite bodies and branch separation. It isn't about how far one part's pattern spreads. **That is why the earlier "opposite trend" comparison was wrong, and it has been withdrawn** (C89).

## Scale 4: whose count of loci?

- **How many loci a pattern covers, and its amount per locus, both change with the observer's motion.** Lengths contract and energy grows.
- **So a rule like "draw when amount per locus falls below ε" gives different answers to different observers.** That quietly picks a preferred frame, bringing back the problem in T6.
- **Relativistic collapse models run into exactly this** (C93, C94).
  - **Pearle's relativistic collapse model (2015):** it builds collapse from a space-time density of particle number, and needs a special time-slicing to stay consistent.
  - **Standard collapse models tie collapse to mass density** (Pearle and Squires, 1994).
  - **Which mass dependence works is still being worked out** (Piccione and Bassi, 2025).

**Whatever sets "too thin" has to be something every observer agrees on.** Counts of events are that kind of thing (D3: only counts are real). Amount per locus is not.

---

## The key point: a smallest unit of *what*?

**Allen's guess, that bandwidth has a smallest unit, is well supported, but in a specific sense.**

- **Single electrons are detected whole,** one at a time, even while their pattern builds up an interference picture (Tonomura, C39). Light is detected in whole photons. So *what a draw produces* comes in whole units. That is standard quantum physics (C97).
- **That same fact rules out using the smallest unit to set "too thin"** (C96). An electron *is* one unit. The moment its pattern spreads over two loci, each locus holds less than one unit. If less than one unit per locus forced a draw, nothing could ever spread, and no interference would exist (T4, T9, T10 fail). A limit much smaller than one unit would work, but then it isn't "the smallest unit" any more. It's just b_min again.

**So the picture that fits:**
- **Outcomes are quantized:** a draw gives a whole particle.
- **Patterns spread smoothly:** shares per locus are fractions.
- **The smallest unit doesn't decide when a lone pattern draws.**

Outcomes are whole units | 0 | Yes, automatically | **No, for lone particles.** ED's distinctive content would have to come from elsewhere (the budget and gravity, clock ticks, strong fields) |

**Both keep "outcomes come in whole units".** The choice is between a spontaneous-collapse ED (A), which is testable but carries one number that experiments already squeeze hard, and an interaction-only ED (D), which has no extra number but, for lone particles, is indistinguishable from standard quantum physics.
