# What's good: the core ideas are in respectable company

Boiled down to what it commits to, early ED says six things, and each has a serious existing version:

1. **Becoming is basic, and things are stable patterns in it.** That's process philosophy, which runs from Whitehead to a live modern literature. It isn't fringe.
2. **Time's arrow is in the basic law, not just in statistics or a special starting state.** This is a real minority position in philosophy of physics:
   - Smolin and Unger, *The Singular Universe and the Reality of Time*
   - Ellis's "evolving block universe"
   - Maudlin's view that the passage of time is basic
3. **What exists, and its properties, are relational.** This is close to Rovelli's relational quantum mechanics.
4. **Spacetime approximates something discrete underneath.** This is a mainstream motivation in quantum gravity.
5. **Laws are stable regularities, not rules imposed from outside.** This is a live debate (Wheeler's "law without law", Smolin's evolving laws).
6. **Measurement is an irreversible record being made.** Bohr stressed the same irreversible step, and decoherent-histories approaches build on it.

The closest existing match is causal set theory's "sequential growth" model (Rideout and Sorkin, 1999). In it the universe grows one event at a time, irreversibly, and geometry comes from the order of events plus counting them. That is your philosophy with the mathematics already attached. Arriving there on your own means your instinct pointed at a real research program.
