## Where attempt 3 started

**Attempts 1 and 2 ended under their own exit rules.** ED's rule could *host* a chance-chosen hand, but its own pieces didn't *make* one.

Attempt 2 had also raised a bigger question. Theories like ED reproduce the right *kinds* of behaviour, but not the particular numbers of physics. **Attempt 3 asked: what in a theory like ED could pin down specific numbers?** That's the "source of specificity".

## Part 1: where specific numbers come from (notes 1–2)

**The known ways, from the literature** (C1–C11):

| way | example |
|---|---|
| **An attractor:** a coupling is pulled to the same value almost whatever it starts at | The top quark's coupling |
| **Sitting at an edge** | Our vacuum sits near the edge of stability |
| **A special value forced at very high energy** | A Higgs mass of 126 GeV, obtained before the 2012 measurement of about 125 |
| **Consistency carving a region, with the real theory at its corner** | The 3D Ising model sits exactly at a kink of the conformal bootstrap's allowed region |
| **Symmetry and counting** | Angles, whole numbers |
| **No source: a huge menu, one picked by selection** | The string landscape |

**Allen's frame: "the attractor is the shape"** (D1). Specific values sit at special points of what's allowed, the way the best exponents sit on the edge of Tao's database. **The literature supports two routes like this** (C12):
- consistency alone puts a theory at a corner;
- dynamics carries it to an edge.

In Allen's relation–gradient–boundary terms, **the gradient ends on the boundary.**

**Two things about ED came out of this:**
- **ED's specific results so far all came from constraints** (C5): forced probabilities, a unique fair coin, whole-number windings, a capped feedback.
- **ED's "capacity of 10¹²²" can't explain anything** (C13). It's the cosmological constant written differently, taken from observation.

## Part 2: the shape of ED's coins (note 3)

**ED's own rules for the coin** (conserve amount, coin then move, mirror symmetry) **are all equations.** Equations carve smooth surfaces, and smooth surfaces have no corners (C16).

**But the coin's odds table has a real shape** (C14, C15, C17, C19). Only some odds tables can come from a coin; a triangle test decides which. With mirror symmetry the shape has just two numbers.

| coin | where it sits |
|---|---|
| **Fair coin** (every outcome 1/3) | **The centre** of the shape |
| **Grover coin** (channels alike) | **An edge:** the farthest a channels-alike coin can go |

- **The fair coin's 120° phases come straight from the shape.** At the centre the three terms of each triangle are equal, so the triangle is equilateral, and its sides must turn 120° apart. That's the plain origin of Allen's 120° hint. It's known mathematics.
- **A correction to attempt 2** (C18, C19). Attempt 2 said the plain Fourier coin was the only fair coin needing no chosen number. There's a second one, fair *and* channels-alike, at φ = 120°.

**All five computed checks came out as written down beforehand.**

## Part 4: a full locus and black holes (note 5)

**ED's rules plus the cap give a black hole with no horizon and a saturated core** (C30):

| | ED with a full locus | general relativity |
|---|---|---|
| Shadow | **4.6% larger** | — |
| Deepest redshift | **a factor of e ≈ 2.72** | unlimited |

**These were ED's first specific physics numbers from its own rule.**

**Checked against observations:**
- **Black-hole images:** not ruled out. ED's shadow is 1 to 1.4 standard deviations from the Sgr A* measurements (C26).
- **Missing surface glow:** decisive against it.
  - **The evidence:** without a horizon, infalling matter's energy has to come out. Sgr A* allows under 0.4% of its accretion power from any surface (C27), and black-hole X-ray binaries are far dimmer than neutron-star ones (C28).
  - **The escape check** (C33), under an exit rule confirmed beforehand: even the reading kindest to ED lets out **10%**, about 25 times the limit.
- **Gravitational-wave echoes:** not decisive (C29).

**So ED's strong-field line closed under its exit rule** (C34): a full locus with ED's current rate law conflicts with the horizon evidence.

**What would have to give** (not tried, by the rule):
- **the rate law,** which was matched to Mercury, not derived;
- **using the far-field gravity strength all the way down to the core;** or
- **the full-locus meaning itself.**

- **A correction to attempt 2:** there are two parameter-free fair coins.

**Open:**

## Allen's ideas at the close (D3, D4)

**"In a discrete picture, gravity doesn't extend forever. There is a floor."** (D3)
- **The idea:** if everything comes in steps, gravity's reach could stop somewhere.
- **What it has to respect:** attempt 1 found gravity has to reach unweakened over at least 10¹⁶ metres (from the graviton-mass bound). A floor would have to sit beyond that, or below what can be measured.

**"Loci are on or off. My body occupies some loci; they are on, committing to other loci my body is on."** (D4)
- **How it differs from attempt 1:** attempt 1 treated a locus's budget as a smooth amount from 0 to 1. In Allen's picture a committed locus is simply **on**, and commitment is a relation among the on-loci of one body.
- **How it connects to this attempt:**
  - **the cap stops being a special edge:** every on-locus is full;
  - **one on-locus is the natural smallest step,** which fits the floor;
  - **graded gravity would have to come from counting on-loci.** That's exactly the counting route this attempt left open.
- **Caution:** the black-hole conflict came from "full" plus the current rate law. An on/off picture makes "full" automatic, so any new line would have to face the same horizon evidence, fixed first in its exit rule.

## Part 5: Allen's picture, worked through (notes 6–7)

**What fits** (note 6):
- **On/off loci explain how things slow near mass.** A process can only happen at a locus that's off, and each distant mass has a tiny independent chance of switching it on. So:
  - clocks slow by e^(−Φ), the chance nothing switched it on;
  - motion slows by the square, since a hop needs two loci off.

  **Those are attempt 1's two matched gravity rules,** now consequences of a meaning (C38).
- **A hard floor fails** (C37). Earth's gravity is about 7 × 10⁻¹⁰ of one event per locus per tick, so a floor at one event would erase everyday weight. Discreteness survives as graininess, or as a floor far too deep to see.

**What doesn't** (note 7):
- **Exclusion can't make gravity feed back on itself** (C42–C44). Slowing the spread of influence breaks Mercury, and slowing sources makes the Moon test worse. **Exclusion subtracts; general relativity's feedback adds.** ED still needs general relativity's weights by hand.

**A correction from Allen: a particle is many loci** (D5, C45). Note 6 had slipped into treating a particle's mass as sitting on one locus. With a particle as many on-loci:
- **Gravity per locus is tiny.** Being on is not the same as holding a full budget of gravity.
- **A full locus is ordinary.** It's closer to **matter excluding matter** than to gravity saturating.
- **"On" has two meanings that must be kept apart:** on because a body is there, and on as gravity's influence.
- **Unaffected:** notes 5 and 7's negative results.
